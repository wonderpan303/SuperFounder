Your first one will be your worst one.

So let's get it out of the way. Today.

What's actually happening

You're waiting to feel ready. Ready doesn't come before the rep, it comes from the rep. Confidence is built from evidence, and the only way to collect evidence is to hit record while you're still a little nervous. Nervous and capable can sit in the same body.

30 seconds

Shake it loose

Stand up. Shake your hands, then arms, then legs for about 20 seconds. Then stop completely and feel both feet on the floor for 10. You're not trying to get calm. You're burning off the extra so the rest can come with you.

0:30
1 minute

Turn it on before you're ready

Turn your camera on and look at yourself. Hit record a few seconds before you plan to speak. Let your nervous system settle into it: this is how I look, this is how I feel, this is how I sound.

Then move your eyes to the lens and say "Hello, this is practice" three times. Slower each time.

Why I hit record with the music on

Put your favorite song on before you start. It tells your nervous system this is a party, not a threat. Nothing to be scared of here. Then flirt with the lens a little.

1 minute

Record your worst one on purpose

Hit record and finish this sentence out loud, one take:

"The thing most people get wrong about [your field] is…"

  • One take. No restart.
  • Save it. Don't delete it for at least 30 minutes.
  • Don't rewatch yet. This rep is about finishing, not judging.
That's rep one. It only goes up from here.
2

Let them.

They get to be right for exactly as long as it takes you to get better.

Body first, then the story

When judgment feels like danger, arguing with your thoughts won't win. Slow the body down first, then shrink the fear to its actual size. It's usually a lot smaller once you say it out loud.

1 minute

Hand on heart. Sigh it out.

Amped, frantic, performing? The words bounce right off, and they feel the unease. Calm reads as nothing to prove, nothing to hide. Nervous means bring the energy down, not up.

Hand on your heart. In through your nose for 4, hold, then one big sigh out. Follow the circle. If you get light-headed, go back to normal breathing.

1 minute

Feet, shoulders, jaw

  1. Press both feet into the floor for five seconds. Let go.
  2. Pull your shoulders up to your ears. Hold three seconds. Drop them.
  3. Let your teeth come apart and your tongue rest. Hum softly for one breath.

Tension in these three spots is what makes a voice sound tight on camera.

2 minutes

Name it. Then let them.

What's the worst thing someone could think when they see your video? Write it in plain words. Then stamp it.

    Now say it out loud. All of it.

    Let them have their opinions. Let them judge.

    I'm such a badass for stretching my boundaries.

    I deserve the bigger things on the other side.

    They can have the opinion. You keep the future.

    It was never about you.

    The second you make it about them, the nerves have nowhere to live.

    What yapping actually is

    Yapping is simply using what you know to change someone else's life. One shift, for one person, in that moment.

    Nerves feed on self-monitoring: how do I look, how do I sound, what will they think. Every drill here moves your attention off your face and onto that one person.

    Becoming magnetic.

    Understanding the psychology of it set me free. Our voice has a personality, and it changes how people perceive us.

    Psychology

    Make them feel seen

    An accurate read of their inner world beats trying to sound smart. Name the feeling your ideal follower is in, and lead with what drives them: pain, fear, dreams, desires.

    Structure

    Think clearly

    One core idea per video, so you speak clearly. Say it in one sentence before you film.

    Scripting

    You become the hook

    This is where we start playing a different game than everybody else. Your yap card below is where it starts.

    1 minute

    Come back to the room

    Out loud, name:

    • Three things you can see ("blue mug", "window", "my notebook")
    • Two things you can feel (chair under you, feet on the floor)
    • One sound in the room

    You're in a room. Not in an imagined courtroom full of critics.

    3 minutes

    Build your yap card. Every time.

    Do this before every video you record. One person. What they're feeling. One core idea. Three bullets, no script.

    Slow downenough to letevery wordhave its place.

    Stop racing to the end of the sentence. Punch the words that matter and let them sit. Enunciate like the news anchors do, but stay natural. No weird news voice.

    1 minute

    Record it to them

    • Music on. Camera on. Hit record a few seconds early and let yourself settle in.
    • Put their name, or a photo of someone who needs this, right beside the lens. Then lock in on it.
    • Open by naming what they're feeling. That's your hook.
    • Talk like it's a voice note to a friend. Three bullets. One take.
    That's what yapping is. You gave something away.

    The whole routine at a glance

      If you freeze mid-take

      Stop. Breathe out once. Look at your first bullet. Say "The main point is…" and keep going.
